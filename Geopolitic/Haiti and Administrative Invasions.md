# Haïti & Administrative Invasions (1915 – Present)

Since the early 20th century, Haiti has experienced a continuous sequence of external administrative interventions, financial takeovers, intelligence operations, and foreign diplomatic mandates. 

While public narrative often attributes Haiti’s ongoing instability solely to internal failure, historical research demonstrates a structural pattern of **administrative engineering**—where foreign banking entities, foreign intelligence agencies, multilateral missions, and political elites have repeatedly robbed Haitian national sovereignty.

---

> [!KEY CONCEPT] Administrative Engineering
> The substitution of a country self-governance with external control, international advisory boards, and proxy political decision makers. Rather than traditional colonization, administrative invasion operates through banking debt control, constitutional manipulation, paramilitary intelligence leverage, and diplomatic influence.

---

# 1. Wall Street & The 1914 Banking Takeover

The financial surrender of Haiti to US banking preceded military intervention:

- **The BNRH Stake Acquisition (1910)**: Under the guidance of National City Bank of New York executive Roger L. Farnham, a syndicate of American banks acquired a controlling interest in the *Banque Nationale de la République d'Haïti* (BNRH). The bank served as Haiti’s treasury, collecting customs duties and issuing currency.
- **The 500,000$ Gold Reserve Seizure (December 17, 1914)**: Under instructions from the US State Department and National City Bank, US Marines landed in Port-au-Prince, entered the BNRH vaults, and confiscated **$500,000 in Haitian gold reserves**. The gold was loaded onto the gunboat USS *Machias* and transported to National City Bank vaults in New York to guarantee debt servicing to American creditors.
- **Financial Receivership**: The gold seizure effectively paralyzed the Haitian state's monetary sovereignty, creating a liquidity crisis that served as the immediate justification for military invasion seven months later.

# 2. The US Military Occupation & The 1918 Constitution (1915–1934)

On July 28, 1915, following the assassination of President Vilbrun Guillaume Sam, US Marines landed in Port-au-Prince, initiating a 19-year military occupation:

- **Customs Control & Revenue Diversion**: US officials seized control of all customs houses, which generated over 80% of Haitian state revenue. Funds were prioritized to pay off American and French bondholders before any allocation to domestic public services or education.
- **The 1918 Constitution Rewrite**: The occupation authority dissolved the Haitian National Assembly when it refused to pass a new constitution. The 1918 Constitution—drafted with direct involvement from Franklin D. Roosevelt (then Assistant Secretary of the Navy)—**repealed Article 5 of the 1804 Constitution**, which had banned foreign land ownership since independence.
- **The Corvée System & Caco Uprising**: The military administration re-enacted the *corvée*—an 1864 law forcing Haitian peasants into unpaid labor to build military roads. This sparked the **Caco Uprising**, led by commander **Charlemagne Péralte**. Over 15,000 Haitians were killed during the counter-insurgency before Péralte was betrayed, executed in 1919, and photographed pinned to a door as a warning.
- **Creation of the Gendarmerie**: The occupation established the *Gendarmerie d'Haïti* (later the *Forces Armées d'Haïti* / FAd'H), creating a militarized internal police force designed to maintain domestic order rather than defend national borders against foreign threat.

### 2.1 The Second Caco War (1918–1920) & The Brutal Event of May 18–19, 1920

The armed resistance against the US occupation reached its peak during the **Second Caco War**, led by supreme commanders **Charlemagne Péralte** and **Benoît Batraville**:

- **The Caco Resistance**: Outraged by the re-imposition of forced labor (*corvée*) and foreign land appropriations under the 1918 Constitution, over 40,000 Haitian peasants joined or supported the Caco guerrilla forces against the US Marine Corps and the Gendarmerie d'Haïti.
- **Péralte's Crucifixion & Batraville's Command**: Following the October 31, 1919 betrayal and assassination of Charlemagne Péralte (whose corpse was photographed tied to a door to resemble a crucifixion and distributed across Haiti as counter-insurgency propaganda), **Benoît Batraville** assumed supreme command of the Caco forces, continuing anti-occupation operations in the Central Plateau and Artibonite regions.
- **The Brutal Event of May 18–19, 1920**: On **May 18–19, 1920**, US Marine Captain Hermann H. Hanneken and Marine patrols ambushed Batraville's encampment near Mirebalais. Batraville was shot and killed on May 18, 1920. US Marine forces took his body, photographed it, and publicly displayed it in Mirebalais before burying it in an unmarked grave to break the psychological spirit of Haitian national resistance.
- **The Tragic Historical Paradox of May 18**: A profound historical irony is that **May 18** is celebrated nationwide in Haiti as **Haitian Flag Day** (*Fête du Drapeau*—commemorating the creation of the blue-and-red flag by Jean-Jacques Dessalines and Catherine Flon at Arcahaie in 1803). On **May 18, 1920**, while the occupied nation observed its national flag holiday, US Marine occupation forces executed and publicly desecrated the body of the last supreme commander of active anti-occupation resistance, effectively ending the Second Caco War while many citizens were unaware of the military execution taking place in Mirebalais on that exact date.

# 3. Cold War Dictatorship & Intelligence Alignment (1957–1986)

During the Cold War, foreign policy toward Haiti prioritized anti-communist alignment in the Caribbean over democratic governance:

- **The Duvalier Regimes**: François "Papa Doc" Duvalier (1957–1971) and his son Jean-Claude "Baby Doc" Duvalier (1971–1986) built an authoritarian dictatorship sustained by the *Tonton Macoutes* (Volontaires de la Sécurité Nationale).
- **Geopolitical Leverage**: Situated less than 60 miles from Fidel Castro's Cuba, the Duvalier regime leveraged its anti-communist stance to secure millions of dollars in US military and economic assistance despite widespread extrajudicial killings and repression.
- **Economic Siphoning**: Millions in foreign aid funds were siphoned into personal offshore accounts while domestic agricultural production collapsed under structural adjustment mandates, initiating rural-to-urban mass migration into Port-au-Prince slums.

# 4. The 1991 Coup, FRAPH, & Intelligence Assets (1991–1994)

In December 1990, Jean-Bertrand Aristide won Haiti's first democratic election with 67% of the vote. Eight months into his presidency, he was overthrown in a bloody military coup on September 30, 1991:

- **The Military Junta**: Led by General Raoul Cédras and Chief of Police Michel François, the junta executed thousands of Aristide supporters.
- **FRAPH & Emmanuel Constant**: The *Front pour l'Avancement et le Progrès d'Haïti* (FRAPH), a death squad paramilitary network, was formed under **Emmanuel "Toto" Constant**. Official US government records later confirmed that Constant was a paid informant for the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) throughout the junta period.
- **Operation Uphold Democracy (1994)**: Under intense refugee pressure, 20,000 US troops landed in Haiti in September 1994 to restore Aristide. However, restoration was conditioned on implementing strict neoliberal economic reforms (tariff reductions on foreign rice) and the complete disbandment of the Haitian Armed Forces (FAd'H).

# 5. The 2004 Bicentennial Ouster & The Ottawa Initiative

As Haiti prepared to celebrate the 200th anniversary of its 1804 independence, international political pressure intensified:

- **The Bicentennial Restitution Demand (2003)**: President Aristide formally demanded that France reimburse Haiti the **1825 Independence Indemnity** (150 million gold francs extorted under threat of war), calculated at **$21.7 billion USD** in modern valuation.
- **The Ottawa Initiative on Haiti (January 2003)**: High-level officials from Canada, the United States, France, and the OAS met in Ottawa, Canada. Reports published by journalist Michel Vastel revealed discussions regarding the removal of Aristide and the potential implementation of a UN trusteeship over Haiti.
- **The 2004 Rebellion & Exile**: In February 2004, an armed insurgency composed of former FAd'H soldiers and FRAPH paramilitary leaders (Guy Philippe, Jodel Chamblain) launched an attack from the Dominican border. On February 29, 2004, US diplomats and security personnel escorted Aristide onto an unmarked American aircraft, flying him into exile in the Central African Republic. Aristide described the event as a "modern-day kidnapping."

# 6. Multilateral Control: MINUSTAH & The "NGO State" (2004–2017)

Following the 2004 ouster, formal administration of Haiti was transferred to international multilateral organizations:

- **MINUSTAH Occupation (2004–2017)**: The United Nations Stabilization Mission in Haiti deployed thousands of foreign military troops and police. 
- **The 2010 Cholera Outbreak**: In October 2010, UN peacekeepers stationed at the Mirebalais base discharged untreated sewage containing *Vibrio cholerae* into the Meille River, a tributary of the Artibonite. The resulting epidemic killed over **10,000 Haitians** and infected more than 800,000. The UN denied responsibility for six years before issuing a partial acknowledgment in 2016.
- **The Interim Haiti Recovery Commission (IHRC)**: Following the catastrophic 2010 earthquake, the IHRC—co-chaired by former US President Bill Clinton—was created to oversee $10 billion in international relief funds. The IHRC bypassed Haitian government ministries, allocating over 90% of aid funds directly to foreign contractors, NGOs, and multilateral agencies, earning Haiti the title of the **"Republic of NGOs."**
- **Electoral Intervention (2010/2011)**: Following disputed first-round presidential election results in 2010, an Expert Mission from the Organization of American States (OAS) pressured the Haitian Provisional Electoral Council (CEP) to reverse vote tallies, removing government candidate Jude Célestin from the runoff and placing pop musician Michel Martelly into the presidency.

# 7. The "Core Group", Mercenaries, & Modern Security Engineering (2017–Present)

In recent years, foreign intervention has evolved into direct governance by informal ambassadorial councils and private security interest networks:

- **The "Core Group"**: Composed of ambassadors from the United States, France, Canada, Brazil, Germany, Spain, the European Union, the UN, and the OAS, this body functions as an un-elected diplomatic steering committee over Haitian politics.
- **Assassination of President Jovenel Moïse (July 7, 2021)**: President Jovenel Moïse was assassinated in his private residence by a squad of 28 foreign mercenaries, primarily Colombian ex-military contractors and Haitian-Americans linked to Florida-based security firm CTU Security.
- **The Appointment of Ariel Henry**: Ten days after Moïse's assassination, on July 17, 2021, the **Core Group issued a formal press release** calling for Ariel Henry to form a government, effectively designating him as de facto Prime Minister without parliamentary confirmation or constitutional mandate.
- **Gang Weaponization & Private Security Interests**: Under the de facto Henry administration, gang alliances (such as the G9 Family and Viv Ansanm) expanded control over 80% of Port-au-Prince, blockading the Varreux fuel terminal and commercial ports. These crises have been leveraged to secure international backing for commercial infrastructure development, mineral access, and private security operations (including discussions involving private military contractors such as Erik Prince's security groups).
- **The Multinational Security Support (MSS) Mission (2024)**: Following Ariel Henry's resignation in April 2024, a foreign-negotiated **Transitional Presidential Council (TPC)** was established, accompanied by a UN-authorized, Kenyan-led Multinational Security Support mission to police Haitian territory.

# 9. The Sovereign Autonomy Synthesis

The century-long history of foreign interventions in Haiti yields a clear political insight: **external administrative engineering does not resolve systemic instability—it perpetuates it.**

Each intervention—whether framed as debt collection, counter-insurgency, anti-communism, humanitarian relief, or gang suppression—has consistently dismantled local public institutions, empowered corrupt proxy elites, and undermined Haitian self-determination.

> [!KEY TAKEAWAY] The Path to Haitian Sovereignty
> Restoring long-term stability to Haiti requires ending the informal governance of external actors like the Core Group, auditing international reconstruction funds, dismantling proxy political networks, and respecting the constitutional right of the Haitian people to construct their own sovereign political and economic institutions without external interference.
